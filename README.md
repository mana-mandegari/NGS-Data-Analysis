## Genetic Investigation of Intellectual Disability and Hearing Impairment in a Consanguineous Family

Mana Mandegari

Affiliation: [Genomics Comprehensive Research Laboratory](https://www.genomicslaboratory.ir/) (Iran), [Helix Institute for Medical & Biological Sciences](https://www.helixinstitute.us/) (USA)

**Background:**  
In a consanguineous family, three of five offspring have significantly low head circumference, consistent with microcephaly. The affected individuals exhibit variable clinical manifestations, including learning difficulties, aggressive behaviour, developmental delay in the first offspring, and hearing impairment in the second offspring. The parents are first cousins and are clinically healthy, with no reported history of intellectual disability or hearing impairment.

**Methods:**  
A list of genes and variants associated with intellectual disability, microcephaly, and hearing impairment was compiled using the ClinVar and OMIM databases. The results were then filtered to include only the selected genes and variants.  
Since the parents of the affected individual are healthy and presumed to be carriers, the disease most likely follows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. Therefore, the affected individual is expected to be homozygous for the disease-associated variant (GT: 1/1), and a filter was applied to the GT column to retain only homozygous variants.   
Variants classified as benign or likely benign in the ClinSig and InterVar (automated) columns were excluded, leaving pathogenic, likely pathogenic, and variants of uncertain significance (VUS) for further analysis. Variants with a high population frequency were also excluded by applying an ExAC-ALL allele frequency threshold of \<0.01.

After applying all filtering criteria, only one exonic frameshift deletion in the *CKAP2L* gene remained. The selected variant was subsequently further evaluated using relevant databases and published literature.

According to the OMIM database, *CKAP2L* is associated with Filippi syndrome, which follows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern. Clinical features of Filippi syndrome include microcephaly, intellectual disability, developmental delay, and, in some cases, ear abnormalities, such as anteverted ears with a hyperconvoluted helix, which may contribute to conductive hearing loss (Chalwade et al., 2025). A search of the literature using the terms “Filippi syndrome” and “hearing loss” identified multiple research articles supporting an association between Filippi syndrome, hearing impairment and even behavioural issues (Faheem et al., 2026).

ClinVar classifies the identified variant, c.968\_972del (p.T323fs), as a pathogenic germline variant associated with Filippi syndrome. The variant is predicted to result in nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) and subsequent loss of protein function. UCSC demonstrated a relatively high degree of sequence conservation across vertebrates, while Ensembl further classified the variant as a protein-altering variant.

**Results:**  
Gene Name: *CKAP2L*  
Variant Type: NM\_001304361:exon5:c.968\_972del:p.T323fs, Frameshift deletion  
Disease Phenotype: Filippi syndrome  
Phenotype MIM Number: 272440  
ClinVar Classification: Pathogenic ⭐

**Conclusion:**  
In this case, a frameshift variant (exon 5: c.968\_972del; p.T323fs) in the CKAP2L gene was identified as the likely cause of Filippi syndrome in the affected individuals. CKAP2L-related Filippi syndrome follows an autosomal recessive inheritance pattern, and the affected individuals inherited two copies of the disease-associated variant, one from each carrier parent. Filippi syndrome is characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, aggressive behaviour, and microcephaly, with conductive hearing loss reported in some cases.
